Before we build anything for Compass, we write two short documents: the press release we'd put out on launch day, and the FAQ we'd expect people to ask. Amazon calls this a PRFAQ. We call it a useful way of finding out that an idea is half-baked before we've spent a fortnight on it.

Writing the announcement first forces a few awkward questions early. Who is this for? What can they do afterwards that they couldn't before? Can we explain it in a paragraph without hiding behind jargon? If the press release is dull or vague, the feature probably is too.

## Why publish them?

Most of these documents would normally stay internal. We'd rather put them here, for three reasons.

- **You can tell us early.** If a planned feature solves the wrong problem, we'd much prefer to hear it before we build it.
- **It keeps us honest.** Each post carries a status: planned, building, shipped or shelved. When something ships, you can compare it with what we said it would do.
- **Compass works the same way.** For larger changes it starts from an intent document rather than a prompt. It would be odd to build it any other way.

We only publish the outward-facing parts. Internal questions about cost, staffing and the less polished trade-offs stay with us.

If we change our minds, the post stays up, marked shelved, with a line on why. Quietly deleting it would rather defeat the point.
